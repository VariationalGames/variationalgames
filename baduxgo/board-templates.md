# Printable Board Templates

Want a physical Badux board? These are full-scale (1:1) printable templates for all three standard
sizes. Print one, tape the sheets together, and you have a paper board you can play on with ordinary
Go stones — or a marking template to lay out a wooden board.

Everything on this page is free to use, for personal or commercial purposes.

## Drawing a hex board is easier than it looks

The board is usually drawn as a honeycomb of hexagonal cells, which sounds like a nightmare to draw
by hand. It isn't, because a honeycomb is just the *dual* of a triangular lattice. Every Badux board
can be drawn exactly the way a Go board is drawn — as straight lines with stones on the
intersections — except with three families of parallel lines at 60&deg; instead of two at 90&deg;.

That turns out to be a very forgiving construction, because **every line runs from one edge of the
board to the other.** So the whole layout is:

1. Draw the outer hexagon. (A compass does this for you: with the compass set to the hexagon's side
   length, step it six times around a circle of that same radius.)
2. Mark N evenly spaced points along each of the six edges. On a 12&times;12&times;12 board that is
   66 marks, all of them on straight edges you can lay a ruler against.
3. Rule straight lines connecting corresponding marks on opposite edges.

Every interior intersection falls out of step 3 on its own, with no interior measuring at all. And
the six outermost grid lines *are* the outer hexagon, so you never draw the outline twice.

## Choosing the spacing

A Japanese Go board uses a 22.0mm &times; 23.7mm grid with stones about 22mm across. A hex grid only
has one spacing to pick, so you have to choose a single number, and the interesting range is 22mm to
24mm.

There is a reason to lean toward the loose end. On a square grid only four neighbours sit at the
grid spacing; the diagonals are 1.41 times further away, so stones never crowd corner to corner. On
a hex grid **all six neighbours sit at exactly the spacing**, so a stone with a full set of
neighbours is touching on every side at once. It plays fine, but dense positions are fiddly to pick
apart.

23mm is close to traditional proportions. 24mm is roomier and more forgiving of a hand-made board.
Templates are offered in both.

## Dimensions

The **grid** column is the distance from outer point to outer point, which is what you measure when
marking out a board. The **board** column adds the 18mm border, and is the size of the finished
hexagon.

| Board | Points | Spacing | Grid (corner to corner &times; flat to flat) | Board with 18mm border |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6&times;6&times;6 | 91 | 23mm | 230 &times; 199mm | 272 &times; 235mm |
| 6&times;6&times;6 | 91 | 24mm | 240 &times; 208mm | 282 &times; 244mm |
| 8&times;8&times;8 | 169 | 23mm | 322 &times; 279mm | 364 &times; 315mm |
| 8&times;8&times;8 | 169 | 24mm | 336 &times; 291mm | 378 &times; 327mm |
| 12&times;12&times;12 | 397 | 23mm | 506 &times; 438mm | 548 &times; 474mm |
| 12&times;12&times;12 | 397 | 24mm | 528 &times; 457mm | 570 &times; 493mm |

If you want to work the numbers out yourself: for a board with N points along each edge and a
spacing of *s*, the side of the grid hexagon is L = (N&minus;1) &times; *s*, the corner-to-corner
width is 2L, and the flat-to-flat height is &radic;3 &times; L.

## Printing at home

1. Print at **100%**. Turn off "fit to page", "shrink to fit", and any scaling your printer driver
   offers. This is the step that goes wrong.
2. Check the calibration bar at the bottom of each sheet with a ruler. It must measure exactly
   100mm. If it doesn't, the print is scaled and the board will come out the wrong size.
3. Trim each sheet along its dashed edges.
4. Lay each sheet over the one above it and to its left, lining up the grid lines through the 10mm
   overlap band. Tape from behind.

Sheet labels tell you which row and column each page belongs to.

## Other spacings

The templates are generated by a small script, included alongside them, if you want a spacing or a
border that isn't offered above. It needs Python and [pycairo](https://pycairo.readthedocs.io/).

[badux_template.py](/baduxgo/board-templates/badux_template.py)

```
python3 badux_template.py --size 12 --pitch 23.5 --border 20 --page a4 --out board.pdf
```

`--size` is the number of points along each board edge, `--pitch` is the millimetres between
adjacent points, `--border` is the millimetres of board beyond the outer points, and `--page` is
`a4`, `letter`, or `full`.
